PCA, visually

Keep the direction where the data varies most; lose the least by throwing the rest away.

Which line keeps the most?

Goal: describe each 2-d sample by one number, its score c = wT(x − x̄) along a unit direction w, and rebuild it as x̂ = x̄ + cw, losing as little as possible.

Approach: the error e = x − x̂ is at a right angle to w, so Pythagoras splits every sample's spread into a kept part and a lost part. Their sum is fixed by the data, so the least error is the most variance:

maxw wTSw  subject to  wTw = 1

Here S = cov(x) with 1/n, so the kept variance (1/n)∑ ci2 is exactly wTSw. Edit S directly or through its eigenvalues and eigenvectors: the samples reshape to have exactly that covariance.

Eigenfaces: an image is a long vector

Goal: store each image with k numbers instead of its d pixel values (d = 64 · 64 = 4096 for an Olivetti face, three times that in color), and rebuild it.

Approach: the same PCA as the line, in d dimensions. The principal directions w1, w2, … are unit eigenvectors of S (d × d), sorted by eigenvalue λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ …; drawn as pictures they are the eigenfaces.

x̂ = x̄ + c1w1 + … + ckwk,   cj = wjT(x − x̄) ⇒ kept = λ1 + … + λkλ1 + … + λd

The variance along wj is λj, so the kept fraction is a running sum of eigenvalues. PCA runs in your browser on the pixels in [0, 1]; λj match sklearn's explained_variance_ (1/(n − 1)).

Try this

  • Drag w around (or press Sweep w) and watch the histogram's spread and the kept variance. Where is it highest, and how high?
    AnswerAt w = v1, the eigenvector of the larger eigenvalue, and the peak height is λ1. The lowest point is v2 with height λ2, a right angle away. Lagrange gives the same thing: ∇ wTSw = λ ∇ wTw is Sw = λw.
  • Tick show S w. For which w does Sw point the same way as w?
    AnswerOnly along the two eigenvectors (and their negatives): that is the definition Sv = λv. Its length there is λ, the variance along that line.
  • Set S to the lesson's [[5, 2], [2, 2]]. How much variance does the best line keep?
    Answerλ = 6 and 1, so the best line keeps 6 of tr S = 7, about 86%, along v1 = [2, 1]/√5.
  • Make λ1 = λ2. Which line is best now?
    AnswerAll of them: S = λI, so wTSw = λ for every unit w, every line keeps the same, and every direction is an eigenvector.
  • Type an off-diagonal entry bigger than allowed, e.g. S = [[1, 3], [3, 1]].
    AnswerIts eigenvalues are 4 and −2, and a variance wTSw can't be negative, so no data has this covariance. A covariance needs |S01| ≤ √(S00S11).
  • On Guess Who, press Guess who? and add components one at a time. How many do you need?
    AnswerOften only a handful: hats, hair color and beards change many pixels, so they land in the first few eigenfaces. The rebuild is recognizable long before it is sharp.
  • On Guess Who, slide k to the end (23). Why is the rebuild perfect? Then tick hold out and look at a held-out face at the largest k.
    Answer24 centered images span at most 23 dimensions, so there are only 23 nonzero eigenvalues and every training face is exactly x̄ plus a mix of 23 eigenfaces. A held-out face is not in that span: PCA can only rebuild what looks like its training images, so it never becomes perfect.
  • Compare the kept-variance curves. Which set needs the most components to keep 90%?
    AnswerCats, at 78 of 299 (Olivetti 66, emoji 44, Guess Who 16). Cats are photos with any fur, pose and background; emoji share one yellow circle, yet their first component keeps only about 15%, since each emoji's eyes and mouth are its own.
  • In the mixer, push c1 to each end. What does w1 change?
    AnswerWhatever moves the most pixels the most: lighting on Olivetti, the background and fur brightness on cats, hair and hats on Guess Who. The top direction is about variance, not about who the face is.
